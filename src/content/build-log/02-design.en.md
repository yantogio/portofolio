The visual direction was set with a design skill that explicitly bans the usual signs of an AI-made site: purple gradients, glass cards, percentage skill bars, grids of tech logos, and generic phrases.

**The first version** followed the logic of an engineering working drawing: a light background, sharp corners, monospace type, and an industrial orange accent. It followed the rules, but when I saw it live it felt stiff and still read like a document, not like me.

**The second version** started from a different question: what if the homepage told a story? The result:

- the homepage became one journey, from vocational school to graduation, with each project appearing in the year it was built,
- a warm night theme: green-tinted black, bone-coloured text, one amber accent,
- characterful Bricolage Grotesque display type, paired with Hanken Grotesk for text,
- motion with a purpose: headlines rise from behind a mask, a paragraph lights up as you scroll, years stick to the left, and the how-I-work cards stack.

Before coding the second version, visual references for each section were generated first with an image generator (the image-to-code skill workflow), then analysed for composition, colour, and hierarchy.
